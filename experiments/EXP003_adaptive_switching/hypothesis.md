# EXP003 Hypothesis

## Primary hypothesis

A mismatch-responsive controller will produce greater goal accessibility than a random schedule with identical switch timing and count because it leaves unproductive frames and retains a frame once task-valid progress appears.

## Prospective predictions

1. Adaptive-minus-random-replay peak goal activation will be positive on average across the 100 paired seeds.
2. Adaptive switching will spend more steps in the useful frame than random replay.
3. Greater useful-frame dwell will mediate the activation advantage descriptively: seed-level activation differences should have the same sign as useful-dwell differences more often than chance-level equality would suggest.
4. Adaptive performance will remain below or equal to the fixed-useful oracle.

Periodic, random-block, fixed-initial, and mixed conditions are secondary controls. No prediction about adaptive superiority over the mixed condition is primary because EXP002 identified normalization-dependent interference there.

## Disconfirmation

The primary hypothesis is not supported if adaptive switching does not outperform schedule-matched random replay on continuous goal activation, if any difference is explained by unmatched switch timing/count, or if the controller receives goal activation as input to the associative dynamics.

## Interpretation boundary

The controller receives a scalar task-validity evaluation of activated candidates. This is not injected into the content dynamics, but it is stronger supervision than an unguided creativity process. Support would demonstrate evaluation-gated switching, not autonomous discovery without feedback and not human creativity.
