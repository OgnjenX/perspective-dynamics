# EXP004 Hypothesis

## Hypothesis

Evaluation-gated perspective switching has an intermediate productive timescale. Very short dwell prevents a useful frame from accumulating enough evidence to be retained; very long dwell delays escape from unproductive frames and may exhaust the run before the useful frame is reached.

## Prospective primary contrasts

For each evaluation threshold (0.001, 0.005, and 0.02):

```text
mean activation at block lengths 10, 15, 20
    > activation at block length 1

mean activation at block lengths 10, 15, 20
    > activation at block length 60
```

The same two inequalities must hold after pooling thresholds. Continuous peak activation is primary; success rate and useful-frame dwell are supporting measures.

## Secondary prediction

The productive range may shift with evaluation threshold because stricter evidence requirements require longer accumulation. This interaction is descriptive unless a specific shift is preregistered later.

## Disconfirmation

The hypothesis is not supported if performance is monotonic, if an extreme equals or exceeds the intermediate mean, or if the pattern appears only after selecting favorable thresholds or intervals post hoc.

## Interpretation boundary

An intermediate optimum would demonstrate a dwell-time tradeoff in this controlled controller. Because decision timing is externally imposed, it would not by itself establish spontaneous metastability, criticality, bifurcation, or neural creativity.
